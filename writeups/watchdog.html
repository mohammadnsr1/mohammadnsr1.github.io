Watchdog: Physically Grounded Conformance Monitoring for Robotic Agentic AI

A hierarchy of independent agents that checks whether what a robot is doing still matches its mission, and steps in when its behavior drifts.

  • Status
    Ongoing PhD research, working prototype
  • Institution
    Arizona State University
  • Paper
    In preparation for IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters (RA‑L)
  • Interface
    Agent tools exposed over the Model Context Protocol (MCP)

    • The problem

    Foundation model driven robots can plan and act in open environments, but nothing independent checks whether what they are doing still matches the mission. A robot that grades its own work is not a trust layer.

    The idea

    Instead of one model that both acts and judges itself, the system is a hierarchy of agents with separate jobs.

    • Watchdog Fast, always on monitoring of the robot's state and actions against mission intent and constraints. It flags drift and can halt or hold the robot.
    • Judge Slower reasoning with a foundation model. It reviews flagged events, weighs context and decides whether the behavior is acceptable.
    • Orchestrator Owns the mission plan and decides how to recover: replan, retry, ask a human or stop.

    Design principles

    Each boundary between agents has to earn its place, for one of three reasons:

    1. Adversarial independence The actor and the checker are kept separate, so the robot is not grading its own work.
    2. Different timescales Fast monitoring and slow reasoning run at different rates and may need different hardware.
    3. Authority boundaries Legal or certifiable lines of authority that need to stay distinct.

    Agents are extracted gradually from a near monolith as natural seams prove stable, rather than designed top down. Reasoning never blocks real time motion control.

    Implementation

    A working prototype in which the agents act through tools exposed over the Model Context Protocol (MCP). There are no metrics on this page yet. They will be added once the paper is out.

    Why it matters

    Trust is what stands between capable robots and deployment in inspection, aviation maintenance and other safety critical work. This project aims to make that trust something you can check, not just assume.

    What's next

    Submit the paper, "Watchdog: Physically Grounded Conformance Monitoring for Robotic Agentic AI," to IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters (RA‑L), then apply the hierarchy to acoustic inspection robots for aviation maintenance.
