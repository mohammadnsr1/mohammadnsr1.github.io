Manipulation

Robotic Manipulation

Two different robotic arms, two different tasks: a custom manipulator we just finished that finds a ball and a goal by camera and kicks the ball in, and a MyCobot Pro solving a physical maze by sight, paired with a digital twin built in MATLAB. Both come down to the same core loop, perceive the workspace, plan a sequence of moves, and execute them precisely enough that small errors do not compound.

Vision and Kinematics

Ball Shooting Manipulator

A camera guided robotic arm that finds a ball and a goal, then kicks the ball in. A Pixy camera locates the ball and the goal using color and pixel count based distance estimates, and inverse kinematics places a 3D printed leg behind the ball for a timed kick toward the goal.

Role
Perception and kinematics, team of three
Stack
Arduino Mega, Pixy camera, three degree of freedom arm with two added wrist servos, 3D printed leg end effector and goal
Outcome
Consistently found the ball and the goal, positioned the leg behind it, and kicked toward the goal, with most error coming from pixel based distance estimates and angle imprecision rather than the kinematics

Perception and Estimation

Perception, Planning, and Estimation

A semester of ROS 2 assignments that built the base for everything else: publisher and subscriber patterns, TF2 frame broadcasting, reactive LiDAR obstacle avoidance, point cloud based object detection, path planning and control, and Bayes filter tuning for sensor and motion models.

Role
Perception, planning, and system integration
Coursework
ROS 2 pub and sub, TF2, reactive navigation, point cloud processing, path planning, Bayes filters
Applied project
Active Perception for Object Localization, a TurtleBot4 system that repositions itself for a better view before trusting a pose estimate
Outcome
Went from writing isolated nodes for each assignment to owning perception, planning, and integration on a full closed loop robot system, where a wrong Bayes filter tuning or a missed frame transform shows up immediately as bad robot behavior

Vision and Reactive Navigation

Assistive Follower Robot

A TurtleBot4 that follows a specific person by detecting their shoes with a custom trained ResNet18 model on OAK D camera images, then fuses that heading with LiDAR based gap finding to avoid obstacles in real time.

Role
Vision and navigation, team of three
Stack
OAK D depth camera, LiDAR, TurtleBot4
Outcome
Built a working understanding of ROS 2 networking, including Discovery Server and middleware implementations like Fast DDS and Cyclone DDS, and worked around real hardware constraints in networking and vision model deployment

Reactive Driving

F1TENTH Autonomous Racing

A 1:10 scale racecar running automatic emergency braking, PID wall following, and follow the gap obstacle avoidance, the same three behaviors developed first in simulation, then ported to run on the real car.

Role
Individual project, perception, control, and safety
Stack
ROS1 Melodic, LIDAR, Ackermann drive, PID control, LibTorch vision
Outcome
Learned that the algorithm was rarely the hard part, the real work was retuning every constant once real LIDAR noise and real inertia replaced their simulated counterparts

About

How I think and work

How the work is oriented, the problems that interest me, and where I like to contribute.

My PhD work sits at the meeting point of physical sensing and agentic AI. I build systems that let autonomous machines listen to, feel, and reason about the physical world they operate in, rather than relying only on cameras and predictions.

The direction I am pushing toward is giving AI agents a real sense of ground truth about the machines, structures, and environments around them, work that matters most in industries like manufacturing, energy, and infrastructure, where an autonomous system acting on a wrong read can be costly or dangerous.

I am always glad to talk with people building in this space, whether that is a research collaboration, an early conversation about a startup, or an industry team looking to bring real world sensing into their AI systems.

The throughline across all of it is physically grounded intelligence, systems that have to sense, reason, and act in the real, noisy, physical world.

Current Work

What I am building now

Right now I am designing agentic architectures built on foundation models for robotic systems, aimed at making autonomous robots reliable and accountable in how they sense, decide, and act.

Hard Problems

Questions I like to stay with

How the solutions nature has already optimized over millions of years, in how animals sense, move, and adapt, can be translated into better designs for sensors, mechanisms, and decision making in robots.

Working Style

How I like to think and work

I think best across boundaries. I am drawn to interdisciplinary work, and I often look to other fields for ideas. Analogy is my main tool for it: noticing how one domain solves a problem and asking what the same move would look like somewhere else.

Curiosity

What keeps me curious

My curiosity runs wide and likes to wander. Lately it keeps circling flight, space, and biology. I love how the most agile birds fly with so little wasted effort, the design problems behind becoming a multi planetary species, and how unsettlingly clever viruses are at getting their way. I am just as curious about people: the ones who ask odd questions, or who see things from an angle I would never have found alone, are usually the ones I want to talk to.
